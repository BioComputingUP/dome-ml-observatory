Searching and browsing

Why won't an author's full name find their papers?

Authors are stored the way the source literature databases store them — surname plus initials, as in Tosatto SCE — so a full first name has nothing to match against. Type the surname and the initials in capitals and the search recognises the shape, matching that author exactly rather than anywhere their surname happens to appear; the initials are allowed to extend, so Farrell G also finds Farrell GP. A surname on its own works too, it is just broader. This is the least discoverable behaviour on the site, and the one that most often looks like a missing paper when it is not.

How are multiple words treated?

They are AND-ed, not read as a phrase: every word has to appear somewhere in the record, in any order. Matching is anchored to the start of a word, so cell finds "cells" and "cellular" but not "excellent" — which removed about a quarter of the false hits that an unanchored match returns. If you want the words adjacent, quote them: "random forest" is matched as a phrase, and tolerates the inline markup and hyphenation that real titles are full of.

Why do I get far fewer results than the {{ corpus().total | number }} papers you screened?

Because searching starts from the {{ corpus().positive | number }} papers classified as AI/ML methods papers, not the whole screened corpus. The rest were screened and judged not to be AI/ML methods papers, and they are still there — clear the classification filter on the search page to include them. Nearly everyone wants the positives, which is why they are the default rather than a setting you have to find.

Why can one uncommon word be slower than two?

A search of two or more words uses the text index, which is fast. A single bare word deliberately does not, because the index stems words and a lone term has no second word to rescue what stemming drops. For whole words the loss is small, but for the combining forms biologists actually type it is severe: neuro on the index returns about 1,700 records where the slower path finds roughly 26,500, and bioinform returns nothing at all.

So a lone word takes the thorough path and can take several seconds. That is a deliberate trade — recall is never given up for speed — and adding a second word puts you back on the fast path.

Why does browsing stop at page 400?

Paging deeper than 10,000 results means asking the database to sort and skip past a large part of the corpus on every request, which it cannot do reliably at this scale. Rather than fail slowly and unpredictably, the limit is enforced up front. If you need more than the first 10,000 of a result set, narrow the filters — or take the whole corpus in one file from Download, which is faster for you anyway.

The data itself

Are the classifications checked by a human?

Not record by record. Every classification and enrichment in the corpus is LLM-generated. The method behind them was validated against a hand-annotated expert benchmark before being run at scale, so the figures are high-confidence across the corpus as a whole — but no individual record has been reviewed by a curator.

Treat any single record's labels as a strong starting point rather than a verified fact, and cite the paper itself rather than our classification of it. If one is wrong, please tell us — that is the fastest way it gets fixed.

What does "not yet enriched" mean on a record?

Enrichment is the second pass — domain, learning paradigm, model family and model type, drawn from controlled vocabularies. Those fields are defined in schema release v{{ schemaVersion() }} and reserved on every record, but the pass has not been run yet, so they are empty for now.

Nothing is missing or broken — the work simply has not happened. Coverage is reported live on the processing history page and will start climbing on its own once the first batch lands.

A record about my work is wrong, or my paper is missing entirely.

Please tell us. Open a record correction issue or email contact@dome-ml.org with the DOI or PMID, and we will correct or remove it.

A missing paper is usually one of two things: it is in the corpus but classified as not an AI/ML methods paper, or it has not been through a processing round yet — the corpus is reloaded several times a year. See licensing for what we redistribute and on what terms.

How do I cite this in a paper?

Cite the specific release you analysed rather than the site, so your work stays reproducible as the corpus grows. Bulk download carries the release identifier and a ready-made citation block, and licensing & citation sets out what the licence covers.
