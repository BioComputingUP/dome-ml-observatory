Searching and browsing

How do I search for an author?

However you would normally write the name. Authors are stored the way the source literature databases store them, as surname plus initials (Tosatto SCE), but the search recognises the other spellings and matches them to that form: Farrell G, farrell g, Farrell, G., G Farrell and Gavin Farrell all find the same author. Case, dots, commas and the order of name and initials make no difference.

The initials are allowed to extend, so Farrell G also finds Farrell GP, and Silvio Tosatto finds Tosatto S, Tosatto SC and Tosatto SCE alike. Given names are not stored anywhere in the corpus, so a full name is matched by reconstructing the initial rather than by looking the name up. A surname on its own works too; it is broader, and slower.

How are multiple words treated?

They are AND-ed, not read as a phrase: every word has to appear somewhere in the title, abstract or author list, in any order. If you want the words next to each other, quote them: "random forest" is matched as a phrase, and still matches titles that hyphenate it or split it with inline markup. A hyphenated word is matched either way, so single-cell also finds "single cell".

What exactly does a word match?

The whole word and its forms, the way PubMed does: cell finds "cells" and "cellular", predict finds "prediction" and "predicting". It does not match the inside of another word, so dome no longer brings back "domestic". To match word beginnings instead, add a star: neuro* finds neuroimaging, neuroscience and the rest. That takes a slower path through the corpus, a few seconds rather than under one. When no whole word matches at all, the star form is tried for you, and the line under the result count says which happened.

A method name or acronym from the published vocabulary is also searched under its other spellings: svm finds papers that only ever say "support vector machine", gnn those that say "graph neural network", and quoting the full name ("large language model") finds its acronym. Acronyms with another common meaning in the biomedical literature (GBM, MDS, LOF) are deliberately not expanded. Pasting a DOI, PMID or PMCID finds that record directly.

Results are ordered by how well the title matches: a title that opens with your term or carries it as an acronym comes first, then titles containing every word, then matches in the abstract or author list, with more-cited papers ahead within each group.

Why do I get fewer results than @if (corpus(); as c) { the {{ c.total | number }} papers } @else { all the papers } you screened?

Search covers the @if (corpus(); as c) { {{ c.positive | number }} } papers classified as AI/ML methods papers. The rest were screened and judged not to be AI/ML methods papers, and the search page does not reach them. They are kept, and reachable two ways: as one file from bulk download, or through the API’s /api/export, which will hand you the whole screened corpus — or any filtered slice of it — a chunk at a time.

The corpus comes from a Europe PMC search for "artificial intelligence" and "machine learning" as it stood when the dataset was built. Each release is versioned, so a later release covers a wider slice of the literature.

Why is a word-beginning search slower?

Every ordinary search is answered from the text index, in about a second. The index knows whole words, so neuro* — every word that begins with neuro — cannot use it and has to scan the corpus instead, which takes a few seconds. The same is true of a search with the classification filter cleared through the API, which the index does not cover.

Why does browsing stop at page {{ lastPage | number }}?

Paging deeper than {{ resultWindow | number }} results means asking the database to sort and skip past a large part of the corpus on every request, which it cannot do reliably at this scale. At {{ pageSize }} results a page, that is {{ lastPage | number }} pages.

It is a limit on browsing, not on access. To go past it, narrow the filters, take the whole corpus in one file from Download, or use the API’s /api/export — it pages on a cursor rather than an offset, so it has no such ceiling and accepts the same filters as this search page.

The data itself

Are the classifications checked by a human?

Not record by record. Every classification and enrichment in the corpus is LLM-generated. The method behind them was validated against a hand-annotated expert benchmark before being run at scale, so the figures are high-confidence across the corpus as a whole, but no individual record has been reviewed by a curator.

Treat any single record's labels as a strong starting point rather than a verified fact, and cite the paper itself rather than our classification of it. If one is wrong, please tell us.

What does "not yet enriched" mean on a record?

Enrichment is the second pass: domain, learning paradigm, model family and model type, drawn from controlled vocabularies. Those fields are defined in schema release v{{ schemaVersion() }} and reserved on every record, but the pass has not been run yet, so they are empty for now.

Nothing is missing or broken. Coverage is reported live on the processing history page and will start climbing once the first batch lands.

A record about my work is wrong, or my paper is missing entirely.

Please tell us. Open a record correction issue or email contact@dome-ml.org with the DOI or PMID, and we will correct or remove it.

A missing paper is usually one of two things: it is in the corpus but classified as not an AI/ML methods paper, or it has not been through a processing round yet. See licensing for what we redistribute and on what terms.

How do I cite this in a paper?

Please cite the DOME Observatory preprint, with Europe PMC alongside it. The preprint will be posted on arXiv but is still in preparation, so check licensing & citation for the full reference before you submit — it also sets out what the licence covers. Note which release you analysed, too, so your work stays reproducible as the corpus grows; Bulk download describes the releases.
